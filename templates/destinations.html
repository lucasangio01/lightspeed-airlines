Destinations

Naboo

A lush, tranquil world of rolling plains, mirror-still lakes, and underwater cities few outsiders ever see. Naboo is known for its elegant architecture, peaceful reputation, and a surprising habit of ending up at the center of galactic history anyway. Popular with travelers seeking scenery over excitement, though we can't promise the excitement won't find you.

Coruscant

A city that swallowed an entire planet, Coruscant is one endless skyline of towers, traffic lanes, and government buildings stacked on top of each other as far down as anyone's bothered to look. Home to the Senate, the Jedi Temple, and more bureaucracy per square meter than anywhere else in the galaxy. If you like your travel loud, layered, and never quite touching actual ground, this is the stop for you.

Tatooine

A twin-sunned desert world where sand gets absolutely everywhere and the local wildlife has opinions about it too. Home to moisture farmers, podracing legends, and a thriving black market that officially doesn't exist. Not much shade, even less water, but the sunsets are worth the sunburn.

Kamino

A stormy ocean world with no land in sight, just endless rain and towering platforms rising straight out of the sea. Kamino is quiet, orderly, and famously good at keeping secrets, including entire armies, apparently. Pack a raincoat, and don't ask too many questions about the cloning facilities.

Geonosis

A dry, rust-colored world of jagged spires and buzzing insectoid life, best known for its droid foundries and a certain arena that guests are strongly advised to avoid touring. The air is dusty, the locals are industrious, and the underground tunnels go on longer than anyone's bothered to map. Not a leisure destination, but a fascinating stop if you don't mind the occasional execution attempt.

Kashyyyk

A dense forest world of towering wroshyr trees, with entire cities built into the branches hundreds of meters up. Home to the Wookiees, known for their strength, loyalty, and a tendency to solve disagreements by picking things up and throwing them. The canopy views are spectacular, the vines double as walkways, and yes, the lower levels really are as dangerous as the stories say.
